Community Diagnosis

Systematic process of determining health status, problems, and resources of a community — foundation of evidence-based public health planning.

Overview & Core Concepts

Community Diagnosis – in-depth process of finding out the profiles, health status of the community and the factors affecting the present status. It is a cornerstone of community health nursing practice that enables rational allocation of resources.

Purpose

Identify health problems, determine community needs/assets, establish priorities, and provide baseline data for planning, implementation, and evaluation of health programs.

Scope

  • Demographic & social profile
  • Environmental & sanitary conditions
  • Morbidity/mortality patterns
  • Health resources & utilization
  • Community dynamics & power structure

Key Principle: Community participation is essential — the community is not just a setting but an active partner in diagnosis, planning, and action.

Steps in Community Diagnosis (White Book)

  1. Preparation: Define objectives, secure permits (barangay captain, health office), form team, prepare tools (survey forms, spot map).
  2. Ocular Survey / Reconnaissance: Walk through community, observe environment, establish rapport, identify key informants, sketch initial spot map.
  3. Community Assembly / Entry: Conduct meeting with community leaders and residents to explain purpose, encourage participation, and mobilize support.
  4. Data Collection (Survey proper): Use questionnaires, interviews, observation, records review, focus groups. Saturation or random sampling.
  5. Data Processing & Graphing: Organize data, create tables/charts (bar graphs, pie charts, line graphs) for visual interpretation.
  6. Data Analysis: Compute rates/ratios (prevalence, incidence, dependency ratio, etc.), identify health problems, categorize (health deficits, threats, foreseeable crises).
  7. Prioritization: Apply criteria: Nature, Magnitude, Modifiability, Preventive Potential, Salience.
  8. Action Plan Formulation: Set goals/objectives, strategies, resources, timeline, evaluation indicators.
  9. Validation & Feedback: Present findings to community for validation, consensus, and shared decision-making.
"Community diagnosis is not an end but a beginning – it must lead to community action." — NLPGN Standards

Data Collection Techniques & Tools

Key Methods

  • Key informant interview (leaders, health workers, teachers)
  • Household survey (structured questionnaire)
  • Focus group discussions (FGD)
  • Observation (spot mapping, windshield survey)
  • Review of existing records (RHU reports, NSO data, vital statistics)
  • Community assembly/meetings
  • Physical examination / screening (BP, nutrition, etc.)

Essential Tools

  • Spot map with legends (color-coded houses, water sources, health facilities)
  • Survey questionnaire (pre‑tested)
  • Family folder / treatment records
  • Target client lists (FHSIS)
  • Anthropometric tools (weighing scale, height board)
  • Community profile checklist (Annex D, CHN White Book)
Spot mapping directions: North at top; include landmarks (barangay hall, health center, church, stores, communal water sources, toilets).

Data Analysis & Prioritization

Key Rates & Indicators

  • Crude birth/death rate
  • Infant mortality rate (IMR)
  • Maternal mortality ratio (MMR)
  • Dependency ratio
  • Prevalence & incidence rates
  • Population density
  • Sex ratio, literacy rate

Categories of Health Problems (NLPGN)

  • Health deficit – gap between actual & achievable status (e.g., malnutrition, high IMR)
  • Health threat – conditions that promote disease (e.g., open dump, lack of immunization)
  • Foreseeable crisis – stressful events (death of breadwinner, disaster)

Prioritization Criteria (5 factors)

  1. Nature – severity/type (deficit vs threat)
  2. Magnitude – number of people affected (%)
  3. Modifiability – ease of intervention
  4. Preventive potential – ability to control or reduce
  5. Salience – community’s perception of importance

Highest score = top priority.

PHN Role & Community Health Nursing Process

PHN Responsibilities in Community Diagnosis

  • Lead/organize community survey & data gathering
  • Facilitate community assembly & participatory analysis
  • Interpret health data (rates, trends) to LGU and community
  • Prioritize problems using standard criteria
  • Develop nursing care plan / community health plan
  • Implement and evaluate interventions with community
  • Advocate for resources based on evidence
  • Train BHWs in data collection

Community Health Nursing Process (CHN Process)

  • Assessment: Data collection, community analysis, nursing/community diagnosis
  • Planning: Setting objectives, prioritizing, designing interventions (SMART goals)
  • Implementation: Executing plan, mobilizing community resources, health education
  • Evaluation: Outcome measurement, re-assessment, feedback loop

(Source: CHN White Book, Chapter III)

The PHN is both a community organizer and a data‑driven decision maker — community diagnosis empowers the nurse to advocate for the underserved.
